Bets

Positions, loosely held. Last revised July 2026. Writing a thesis down is the cheapest way to find out you're wrong. Nothing here is investment advice — it's a public record of my judgment so I can grade it later.

Theses

1. Value accrues to the layer that owns meaning, not the model.

Models are compounding toward commodity: capabilities converge, prices fall, switching costs stay low. What doesn't commoditize is proprietary data and the semantic machinery that makes it usable — definitions, provenance, governance, distribution. I work on this layer daily, which makes me biased and informed; I'd rather own the toll road between data and agents than the engines driving on it.

2. "Agent-legible" becomes a balance-sheet adjective.

Enterprises whose systems agents can safely read and act on will run structurally cheaper than those where every workflow needs a human translator. The market doesn't price this yet because it's invisible in a demo and only shows up in operating margins two years later. Watch capex-light incumbents with clean data platforms; skip the ones bolting chat onto chaos.

3. Boring infrastructure is the honest AI trade.

The picks-and-shovels framing is a cliché because it keeps being true: data warehouses, observability, identity, payments rails — anything that becomes more load-bearing when software starts writing software. When I evaluate a public company now, my first question is: does agent adoption increase or decrease this company's pricing power?

4. India's formalization is a multi-decade compounder.

I grew up in Mumbai and I've built tooling for doing business in India — scheme discovery, compliance, foreign-investor guidance. The thesis: digitized rails (UPI, GST, ONDC) keep pulling the informal economy into the formal one, and every percentage point of that migration is revenue for exchanges, lenders, and compliance software. Slow, unglamorous, relentless.

Angel & advising

I advise and occasionally invest at the earliest stage. What I look for, in order: a founder who is the customer, a wedge that a big company is structurally unable to copy (not just unwilling), and evidence the team ships weekly. What I bring: pattern-matching from inside Google-scale systems and enterprise data platforms, and honest technical diligence. If that's useful to you, email me.

Studying

I treat investing itself as a subject to learn in public, same as engineering. The current curriculum: reading annual reports of data-platform companies through the agent-legibility lens above, and pressure-testing my theses against the classic value framework — moats, pricing power, owner earnings — to see where the AI era actually breaks the old rules and where it just re-states them. Notes from this study land in notes; graduated conclusions get promoted here.

Open questions

Things I don't have a position on yet — the queue for future theses.

  • Who captures the margin when agents negotiate with agents — the platform, the protocol, or nobody?
  • Is enterprise knowledge-graph work a product category or a consulting business wearing a product costume?
  • What's the first job where an agent's error rate (not capability) is the binding constraint, and who sells the insurance?
  • Does the semantic layer consolidate into the warehouse vendors, or stay a neutral independent layer the way dbt did?
