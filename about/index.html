About

My name is Saheb Singh Gandh. Online I'm bardbyte — bard because I care about the story data tells, byte because someone has to implement it.

I started coding in Mumbai, building things to route around everyday friction. That instinct — software as a lever against friction — has been the through-line since. I did my undergraduate degree in computer science at the University of Mumbai, then a master's in information systems management at Carnegie Mellon (2019), where I also worked with Pittsburgh city officials on ML models for traffic prediction.

From 2020 to 2024 I was at Google — first on Ad Manager in Boulder, then as a senior engineer on Search and Books in Mountain View. The work I'm proudest of there is unglamorous and specific: a metadata-clustering system that cut ingestion time by 86%, a partner-selection model for Google Books that saved about $800K a year, and a copyright-monitoring service that cut non-compliance by 30%. Working on systems that answer billions of queries teaches you a permanent lesson: at scale, the boring parts are the product.

In 2024 I left Google for American Express, where I lead knowledge-graph and semantic-layer work on Lumi, the enterprise data warehouse. The problem obsessing me — at work and in side projects — is making enterprise knowledge legible to AI agents: typed definitions, provenance, governed access. I think it's the highest-leverage unsolved problem in applied AI, and I've put my theses about it in writing.

Outside of work I advise early-stage startups on technical architecture, invest a little, and build tools I want to exist — from agent-session monitors to a daily Hukamnama reader.

What this site is

A working notebook, in the tradition of the personal sites I most admire: essays I'd defend in two years, notes I learned this week, and bets I want to be graded on. It exists to think in public, not to be hired from — though if reading it makes you want to work together, my inbox is open.
